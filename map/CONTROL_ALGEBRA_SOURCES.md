# Control-algebra / behavioral-quotient sources

Snapshot: 2026-08-10.

This bibliography supports `PASS5_CONTROL_ALGEBRA.md` and `PASS6_BEHAVIORAL_QUOTIENT.md`. It is intentionally separate from the older broad `SOURCES.md` so the current seam can be audited without reading the whole KYY map.

## Modern sequence-model algebra

1. **Heo et al. — Why Depth Matters in Parallelizable Sequence Models: A Lie Algebraic View** (2026)  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05573  
   Direct prior art for the statement that the Lie algebra generated by input-conditioned sequence-model generators controls order-sensitive expressivity; uses commutators/Magnus structure and state-tracking experiments.

2. **Shakerinava et al. — The Expressive Limits of Diagonal SSMs for State-Tracking** (ICLR 2026)  
   https://openreview.net/forum?id=5bg5Ru5OML  
   Single-layer diagonal complex SSMs cannot represent non-Abelian group tracking at finite precision; depth corresponds to solvable-group structure.

3. **Grazzi et al. — Unlocking State-Tracking in Linear RNNs Through Negative Eigenvalues** (ICLR 2025)  
   https://openreview.net/forum?id=UvTo3tVBk2  
   Transition spectrum/algebra changes parity and modular-counting capability; products of rank-one reflector-like transitions give broad regular-language capability.

4. **Ebrahimi & Memisevic — Revisiting Bi-Linear State Transitions in Recurrent Neural Networks** (NeurIPS 2025)  
   https://openreview.net/forum?id=xcqrAzYzCj  
   Bilinear hidden/input interactions are explicitly studied as a state-tracking inductive bias. Important boundary against claiming control-affine/bilinear recurrence itself.

5. **Hu et al. — Improving Bilinear RNN with Closed-loop Control (Comba)** (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight)  
   https://openreview.net/forum?id=jlJaRXDzCE  
   Brings closed-loop control language and scalar-plus-low-rank transitions into a modern large-scale bilinear RNN.

## Bilinear / graph controllability

6. **Sontag & Qiao — Further results on controllability of recurrent neural networks** (1999)  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9902023  
   Direct warning that controllability of RNNs is an old research object.

7. **Albertini & D'Alessandro — The Lie Algebra Structure and Nonlinear Controllability of Spin Systems** (2001)  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0106115  
   Lie algebra and interaction-graph structure in networked control.

8. **Burgarth et al. — Local controllability of quantum networks** (2008)  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.3975  
   Shows how local control of a subsystem can imply control of much larger network dynamics under structural conditions.

9. **Godsil & Severini — Control by quantum dynamics on graphs** (2010)  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.5397  
   Dynamical Lie algebra generated from graph matrices; sparse graphs can support universal control/computation under suitable conditions.

10. **Zhang & Li — Analyzing Controllability of Bilinear Systems on Symmetric Groups: Mapping Lie Brackets to Permutations** (2017/2019)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02332  
    Maps Lie-bracket controllability to permutation products and graph connectivity; especially relevant because KYY's benchmark uses permutation composition.

11. **Wang et al. — Controllability and Accessibility on Graphs for Bilinear Systems over Lie Groups** (2020)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11929  
    Very close mathematical skeleton to the KYY toy probe: `SO(N)` dynamics, skew drift, local `B_ij=E_ij-E_ji` controls, drift/control interaction graphs, Lie algebra rank condition. Also gives connected-union examples that remain uncontrollable when controlled components are too small.

12. **Cheng, Zhang & Li — Combinatorics-Based Approaches to Controllability Characterization for Bilinear Systems** (2020)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03430  
    Represents Lie brackets through permutations/graphs and connects to Lie algebra decompositions and representation theory.

13. **Wang et al. — Structural Controllability on Graphs for Drifted Bilinear Systems over Lie Groups** (2021)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12915  
    Structural controllability with prescribed drift/control zero patterns on `SO`, `GL`, and `SU` groups.

14. **Gago-Encinas et al. — Graph test of controllability in qubit arrays** (2022)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04828  
    Graph tests for how many/localized controls are required.

15. **Wiersema et al. — Classification of dynamical Lie algebras of 2-local spin systems on linear, circular and fully connected topologies** (2024)  
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-024-00900-2  
    Boundary conditions/topology change the DLA class; useful context for the path/ring contrast.

16. **Kökcü et al. — Classification of dynamical Lie algebras generated by spin interactions on undirected graphs** (2024)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19797  
    General graph classification; in its setting bipartite versus non-bipartite structure is a major algebraic divider. Not the same representation as the KYY `SO(N)` probe, but an important warning that parity/topology patterns can be symmetry-class phenomena rather than accidents.

## Actuator/control placement as a resource problem

17. **Tzoumas et al. — Minimal Actuator Placement with Optimal Control Constraints** (2015)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.04693  
    Minimal actuator count under controllability/control-effort constraints is already a formal optimization problem.

18. **Guo et al. — Actuator Placement for Optimizing Network Performance under Controllability Constraints** (2019)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08120  
    Network actuator placement with explicit performance/resource objective.

19. **Guo et al. — Actuator Placement for Structural Controllability beyond Strong Connectivity and towards Robustness** (2021)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01850  
    Placement and backup controls under structural-controllability constraints.

**KYY implication:** "choose few control ports" is not new. The remaining question must couple placement to **modern state-tracking behavior and recurrent communication/depth cost**, not merely controllability.

## Automata / behavioral realization

20. **Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav — Extracting Automata from Recurrent Neural Networks Using Queries and Counterexamples** (2017)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.09576  
    Extracts a DFA describing trained RNN behavior.

21. **Rabusseau, Li & Precup — Connecting Weighted Automata and Recurrent Neural Networks through Spectral Learning** (2018)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01406  
    Weighted finite automata and linear second-order RNNs are expressively linked; Hankel/spectral methods provide finite-dimensional linear realization machinery.

22. **Okudono et al. — Weighted Automata Extraction from Recurrent Neural Networks via Regression on State Spaces** (2019)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02931  
    Extracts weighted automata from trained RNN state spaces.

23. **Defourneau & Petreczky — Realization theory of recurrent neural networks and rational systems** (2019)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05609  
    Formal realization/minimality theory for RNN/rational-system behavior.

24. **Wang et al. — Connecting First and Second Order Recurrent Networks with Deterministic Finite Automata** (2019)  
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04644  
    Explicit correspondence between recurrent interaction order and regular-grammar/automata complexity.

25. **Siems et al. — Learning State-Tracking from Code: REPL Traces and Probabilistic Automata** (2026)  
    https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZO92hNK7VC  
    Moves modern state tracking toward sparse next-token supervision and partially observable probabilistic automata.

## Current subtraction

None of these individual statements is a KYY novelty:

```text
Lie algebra matters for sequence order
bilinear RNNs track state
local graph controls can generate global dynamics
control placement can be optimized
RNN behavior can be quotiented/extracted as an automaton
```

The current unmapped bridge remains the **joint resource question**:

> choose a sparse geometry and very small local input-conditioned control interface so that the *behaviorally required quotient algebra* is realizable with short local words and competitive recurrent cost.

That is a search target, not a novelty claim.
